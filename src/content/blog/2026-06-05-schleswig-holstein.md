## Report

A few months before the two-day trip MK wrote me that he might have a long weekend to visit me in
HH. I made a plan for cycling from HH to Schleswig with a sleepover next to Albersdorf. However, two
days before departure MS decided to join us, which signaled that it would be necessary to improvise.

My flat is quite small, so we spent a harsh night at my place and in the morning we departed in
the direction of Wedel. There we had a short breakfast in the yacht harbor after our traditional
visit to a supermarket and then we took a "shize" cycle path through two rotating bridges to Glückstadt.
This used to be an important city, and even the capital of the region. Once it was even conquered by
Albrecht of Valdštejn in the Thirty Years' War. This was my typical Saturday track, so until there everything
seemed to be straightforward. Then we headed to Brunsbüttel. All this region is well known for apple
orchards and many water channels with surrounding long-street villages. And I wanted to show this to
my friends so they could see typical north German rural villages.

The Nord-Ostsee-Kanal starts in Brunsbüttel for medium-size trade and cargo ships to shorten the route
from the North Sea to the Baltic Sea. Part of our following cycle path then led to the small market town Burg
(Dithmarschen region). Therefore, we could observe life on such a water road. There are two
interesting things. One was that we didn't see any cargo ship during our cycling next to the channel. And
we were thinking about what the typical prices are for ships taking this short route, and how it is linked
with current increases in oil costs. The second surprising thing was ferries instead of bridges. In
Brunsbüttel there was one going there and back all the time and then we cycled next to two others on
the way to Burg. What is the cost of personnel, fuel, and ship in comparison to building a bridge
with the necessary high elevation?

In the evening we reached a small but cozy town named Burg and slept in pods. This was a very nice
place to be and also offered some local products like honey. In Germany, the honey jars from local
beekeepers are sold in typically shaped glass, so I knew if I bought one it would most probably be very
good. I was right, I enjoyed it very much. An evening walk around Burg took us to a local scenic point.
This was a rather funny experience due to its location in the middle of a forest with a very narrow view
at a few houses and a field. This closed our day in a good mood.

The next day I wanted to present some of the local prehistoric monuments to my colleagues from Prague.
In the middle of Schleswig-Holstein is a small village called Albersdorf. It is situated just behind
the Kanal and it is surrounded by tombs of old kings and lords. These date to the time of
Doggerland, which is by itself a very interesting story from the North Sea. I discovered it there some
time ago when I had visitors and I was stunned. Firstly, all sites are open, secondly, the village is
surrounded by beautiful oak forest, and thirdly there is a new museum with an open-air site to grasp the
history of the place. I am not sure if my colleagues understood the historical significance of such
a place, but then we decided to change our plans and instead of Schleswig travel to Büsum.

Büsum is a small touristic port on the North Sea and it can be quite crowded by tourists. Its mud
beaches are also UNESCO heritage. The best time for a visit is when there is low tide, when one
can walk on the mud a few hundred meters to the sea. There are many shells, worms, and crabs. This
beach has a feeling like an ancient environment before mammals came up to the land. Unfortunately, we
were not so lucky and when we arrived there was high tide. Anyway, we used the time to walk in the
sea and enjoyed views at the North Sea. It is something that we back home as a landlocked country miss
more than anything.
