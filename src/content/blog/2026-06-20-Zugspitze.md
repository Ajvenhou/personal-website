# Report
Some time ago, I had a vision to climb the Matterhorn (Switzerland). However, I do not have enough
experience to climb it safely at the moment. In the past, I regularly visited climbing walls,
but I never climbed anything above level 7. Moreover, over the last few years in HH, I have mainly been
cycling and running. I really miss mountains here in Hamburg. I shared my wish with one of my
friends (H.), who originates from the Alpine region. He suggested that I test myself first on the
highest peak in Germany---Zugspitze, which is not so difficult to climb for an untrained "climber".

The little expedition started when I arrived in Kempten in Bavaria on Friday evening around 10 pm and
met H., who offered me a place to sleep. I went to sleep around 11 pm and got up around 3:30 in
the morning the next day. After a quick breakfast, we drove to the Hammersbach parking lot. There we
parked the car and started climbing up at 6:20 to the Höllentalanger Hut (1400 m). We wanted to
reach the first Klettersteig section before it became crowded. Also, we were aware that the
weather forecast for the late afternoon indicated a possibility of storms at higher altitudes. I
know from my hometown in the middle of the mountains how quickly weather can change, even at
significantly lower altitudes, and therefore I was very much in favor of the plan of a fast climb up
and a quick descent down through the valleys.

For the first hour and a half, we were on the pedestrian path leading through tunnels and stairs next
to a fast water stream. On the way, there were a few stunning views of several waterfalls and the
strength of the stream falling from the mountains above --- just check the photos below. Also, there
were Alpine salamanders (*Salamandra atra*). H. told me that these are quite rare. Well, I started
counting after approximately the tenth and got to thirty-five (Was it luck? Or are they not so rare
after all? https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpine_salamander). Our decision to climb quickly was
apparently a good one, as we saw immediately when we got to the Höllentalanger Hut that there were
at least another thirty mountaineers who were preparing for the climb. The closer we got to the
first *via Ferrata* section, the more people were around us. These surprising numbers were maybe the
result of the good report about the good climbing conditions on the iceberg ahead of us.

Finally, we reached the first *via Ferrata* section. This was rated as difficulty B. Although
there was maybe a fifty-meter-deep fall below us, it was rather quick to climb through, and we
quickly passed some slower climbers. Then followed another 1-2-kilometer walk along a stone
road up to the 2000 m elevation. Suddenly, I started to feel my lack of acclimatization. My
legs were significantly heavier than I would have expected considering the previous climb. I
significantly slowed down. Finally, at the end of the stony road we reached Höllental --- the last
glacier in Germany (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Höllentalferner). *Note: Later I learnt that there
is another glacier in Germany called Schneeferner https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schneeferner.* I was
lucky that H. lent me shoe spikes; otherwise, it would have been very slippery uphill. Anyway, I slipped
once and went down by 5 m. Next time I will have an axe with me. At the end of the glacier started
the second section of *via Ferrata*, this time labelled C.

After quickly unclipping the shoe spikes, we wanted to start climbing again; however, there was already
a queue of climbers ahead of us! It took roughly 5 to 10 minutes to get on the wall. It was more difficult than
I assumed. The problem was not the wall but that we had to stay there for a very long time waiting for
mountaineers above us to get up. Also, my boots were too large for some of the climbing sections, so I
couldn't rest. Finally, we got up the steep wall and started climbing the hard but less dangerous
part of the klettersteig. It took me two to three hours. The peak looked very close all the time, but it
was hard.

Finally, I noticed that I was 50 meters from the summit. H. had already been waiting for me for 50
minutes! And after a five-minute break, we could get to the cross at the top together (roughly at
12:50), take a photo, and go to the huge tourist building next to the summit. There I had a short
snack and we started to think about what to do next. I even thought that maybe taking a cabin down would not be such a
bad idea. Anyway, after one hour's rest, we felt that we could walk down. I was definitely against the
hard klettersteig, which later proved to be a critically wrong decision. We decided to take an easy way
down via the *via Ferrata* marked A. For the first 2 kilometers, H. was still faster than me, but when we got below
2500 m my legs started to feel normal again and I was again faster.

After roughly 10 kilometers, close to Knorr Hut, we found out that the current route was indeed
technically much easier. However, we had to go another 20 kilometers, which practically meant that we
would have a 40-kilometer expedition that day in quite hard terrain. Well, there was no other option.
Luckily, we did not have to climb any other mountain, and so we were just walking along the Partnach
River to the town of Garmisch-Partenkirchen. I felt surprisingly energetic; however, my hard
boots and almost zero preparation started to take their toll. At the thirtieth kilometer, I started to feel
blisters all over both my feet. Therefore, the last five kilometers were just pure pain. Thank God,
H. was wearing running shoes, which in the end were a much better choice for our fast walk. When I got
to Garmisch-Partenkirchen, we agreed that he would walk faster to the car and I would walk towards the
train station, where he would bring me my large backpack for the way back to HH. Everything went as
agreed; we said farewell, and I took the night train to HH at 10 pm.

The travel back was harder for my fellow companions than it was for me. I tried to clean myself in the
train bathroom, but still---sorry to the lady sitting close to me. Also, throughout Sunday, I was
walking like a cowboy until my blisters healed.

Overall, this expedition was much more than I expected, and I learned far more than I wanted to from
my first true mountaineering experience.

# Quick notes
- Take-home experiences: Support rope for sitting on the wall. Better check of the routes. At least
  one day of acclimatization for mountains around 3000 m.
